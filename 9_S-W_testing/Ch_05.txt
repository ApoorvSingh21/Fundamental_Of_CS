1. WHAT IS AGILE?
====================================================================

Agile is a software development approach in which software is
developed and delivered in small, frequent increments.

Instead of developing the complete application first and testing it
at the end, Agile teams:

    Plan → Develop → Test → Review → Improve → Repeat

The development happens in short cycles called ITERATIONS.

In Scrum, these iterations are commonly called SPRINTS.


Example:

Traditional approach:

Requirements
     ↓
Design
     ↓
Development
     ↓
Testing
     ↓
Deployment
     ↓
Complete Product


Agile approach:

Sprint 1
  ↓
Feature A → Develop → Test → Review

Sprint 2
  ↓
Feature B → Develop → Test → Review

Sprint 3
  ↓
Feature C → Develop → Test → Review

Sprint 4
  ↓
Feature D → Develop → Test → Review


The product gradually becomes more complete.


====================================================================
2. WHY AGILE WAS INTRODUCED
====================================================================

Traditional development models such as Waterfall can become difficult
when requirements change frequently.

For example:

Customer initially says:

    "I want a basic shopping website."

After development starts:

    "I also want online payment."

Later:

    "Add Google login."

Later:

    "Add product recommendations."

Later:

    "Add wishlist."

A rigid development process may make these changes expensive.

Agile is designed to support:

    - Changing requirements
    - Frequent customer feedback
    - Frequent releases
    - Continuous testing
    - Continuous improvement
    - Close team collaboration

4. AGILE MANIFESTO
====================================================================

The Agile Manifesto emphasizes four major values.

1. Individuals and interactions
   over processes and tools

2. Working software
   over comprehensive documentation

3. Customer collaboration
   over contract negotiation

4. Responding to change
   over following a plan


IMPORTANT:

Agile does NOT mean:

    "No documentation"
    "No planning"
    "No process"
    "No testing"

Instead, Agile means that these things are performed in a
flexible and practical way while giving higher priority to
working software, collaboration, feedback, and adaptability.


====================================================================
5. FOUR VALUES OF AGILE
====================================================================

VALUE 1:
Individuals and interactions over processes and tools

Meaning:

People and communication are extremely important.

Example:

Developer and QA directly discuss a defect instead of relying only
on lengthy formal communication.


VALUE 2:
Working software over comprehensive documentation

Meaning:

Documentation is useful, but delivering functional software is
the primary objective.

Example:

Instead of spending excessive time creating documents, the team
focuses on delivering a working login feature.


VALUE 3:
Customer collaboration over contract negotiation

Meaning:

The customer continuously provides feedback.

The team works with the customer to understand changing needs.


VALUE 4:
Responding to change over following a plan

Meaning:

A plan is useful, but the team should be able to adapt when
requirements change.

Example:

If users suddenly require UPI payment support, the product backlog
can be changed and the feature can be prioritized.


====================================================================
6. AGILE PRINCIPLES
====================================================================

The Agile approach is based on principles such as:

1. Satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery.

2. Welcome changing requirements.

3. Deliver working software frequently.

4. Business people and developers should work together.

5. Build projects around motivated individuals.

6. Prefer direct communication.

7. Working software is a primary measure of progress.

8. Promote sustainable development.

9. Continuous attention to technical excellence.

10. Simplicity is important.

11. Self-organizing teams produce better solutions.

12. Teams should regularly reflect and improve.


For QA engineers, especially important principles are:

    - Continuous testing
    - Early feedback
    - Frequent delivery
    - Collaboration
    - Adaptability
    - Continuous improvement

8. WHAT IS SCRUM?
====================================================================

Scrum is an Agile framework used to manage and develop products
iteratively and incrementally.

Scrum organizes development work into short iterations called:

    SPRINTS


Basic Scrum structure:

             PRODUCT
                |
         PRODUCT BACKLOG
                |
         SPRINT PLANNING
                |
          SPRINT BACKLOG
                |
              SPRINT
                |
       +--------+--------+
       |        |        |
    Develop   Test     Review
       |        |        |
       +--------+--------+
                |
         SPRINT REVIEW
                |
      RETROSPECTIVE
                |
          NEXT SPRINT

9. SCRUM ROLES
====================================================================

The important Scrum responsibilities in this syllabus are:

1. Product Owner
2. Scrum Master
3. Development Team

QA engineers commonly work closely with all of them.


====================================================================
10. PRODUCT OWNER
====================================================================

The Product Owner represents the product/business perspective.

Major responsibilities:

    - Understand customer needs
    - Define product requirements
    - Manage product backlog
    - Prioritize backlog items
    - Clarify requirements
    - Define/accept product outcomes
    - Communicate business expectations


Example:

An e-commerce Product Owner may prioritize:

    1. Login
    2. Product search
    3. Shopping cart
    4. Payment
    5. Wishlist


Why?

Because payment may be more important for the upcoming release
than wishlist functionality.


====================================================================
11. SCRUM MASTER
====================================================================

The Scrum Master helps the Scrum team follow Scrum practices.

Responsibilities include:

    - Facilitating Scrum events
    - Helping remove impediments
    - Supporting the team
    - Encouraging collaboration
    - Helping the team improve
    - Supporting effective Scrum implementation


IMPORTANT:

Scrum Master is NOT simply the team's traditional project manager.

The role focuses strongly on facilitating Scrum and helping the
team work effectively.

12. DEVELOPMENT TEAM
====================================================================

The Development Team consists of people who build the product.

Depending on the organization, it can include:

    - Developers
    - QA/Testers
    - Automation Engineers
    - UI/UX specialists
    - Other technical specialists


Responsibilities include:

    - Design
    - Development
    - Testing
    - Integration
    - Fixing defects
    - Delivering working increments


====================================================================
13. SPRINT
====================================================================

A Sprint is a short, fixed development cycle in Scrum.

During a Sprint, the team works on selected backlog items and aims
to produce a usable product increment.

Typical flow:

    Sprint Planning
          ↓
       Sprint
          ↓
    Daily Scrum
          ↓
    Development
          ↓
       Testing
          ↓
    Sprint Review
          ↓
    Retrospective


Example:

Suppose Sprint duration = 2 weeks.

Sprint goal:

    "Implement and test user login."

During the Sprint:

Day 1:
    Planning

Days 1-10:
    Development + Testing + Bug fixing

Daily:
    Daily Scrum

End:
    Sprint Review
    Sprint Retrospective


====================================================================
14. PRODUCT BACKLOG
====================================================================

Product Backlog is a prioritized list of work required for the
product.

It can contain:

    - Features
    - User stories
    - Bugs
    - Improvements
    - Technical work
    - Other product work


Example:

E-commerce Product Backlog:

    1. User registration
    2. Login
    3. Product search
    4. Product details
    5. Shopping cart
    6. Wishlist
    7. Payment
    8. Order tracking
    9. Refund
    10. Notifications


The Product Owner generally manages and prioritizes the backlog.


====================================================================
15. SPRINT BACKLOG
====================================================================

Sprint Backlog is the selected work from the Product Backlog that
the team plans to work on during the current Sprint.

Example:

Product Backlog:

    Login
    Search
    Cart
    Payment
    Wishlist
    Order tracking


For current Sprint:

    Login
    Search
    Cart


These selected items form the Sprint Backlog.


Difference:

Product Backlog:
    Complete prioritized product work.

Sprint Backlog:
    Work selected for the current Sprint.


====================================================================
16. USER STORY
====================================================================

A User Story describes a requirement from the perspective of a user.

Common format:

    As a <type of user>,
    I want <functionality>,
    so that <benefit>.


Example:

    As a customer,
    I want to log in using my email and password,
    so that I can access my account.


Another example:

    As a customer,
    I want to add products to my cart,
    so that I can purchase multiple products together.


====================================================================
17. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
====================================================================

Acceptance Criteria define the conditions that a user story must
satisfy to be considered acceptable.

Example User Story:

    As a customer,
    I want to log in using email and password,
    so that I can access my account.


Acceptance Criteria:

    1. Valid email and password should allow login.
    2. Invalid password should show an error.
    3. Invalid email should show an error.
    4. Empty fields should be validated.
    5. Successful login should redirect to the account/dashboard.
    6. Password should not be displayed in plain text.


QA uses Acceptance Criteria as an important basis for designing
test scenarios and test cases.


====================================================================
18. USER STORY → ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA → TEST CASE
====================================================================

This relationship is very important for QA.


USER STORY
    ↓
What does the user need?


ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
    ↓
What conditions must be satisfied?


TEST SCENARIOS
    ↓
What should we test?


TEST CASES
    ↓
How exactly will we test it?


Example:

User Story:

    Customer wants to log in.


Acceptance Criteria:

    Valid credentials should allow login.


Test Scenario:

    Verify login with valid credentials.


Test Case:

    1. Open login page.
    2. Enter valid email.
    3. Enter valid password.
    4. Click Login.
    5. Verify successful login.


====================================================================
19. DAILY STAND-UP / DAILY SCRUM
====================================================================

Daily Stand-up is a short daily team meeting.

Team members commonly discuss:

    1. What did I do yesterday?
    2. What will I do today?
    3. Are there any blockers/impediments?


Example QA update:

    Yesterday:
    I completed testing for the registration module.

    Today:
    I will start testing login functionality.

    Blocker:
    Test environment payment service is unavailable.


Purpose:

    - Synchronization
    - Transparency
    - Identify blockers
    - Improve communication


It is NOT intended to become a long status meeting.


====================================================================
20. SPRINT PLANNING
====================================================================

Sprint Planning happens before the Sprint starts.

The team determines:

    - What work should be done?
    - What is the Sprint goal?
    - How much work can the team take?
    - How will the work be approached?


Example:

Product Owner prioritizes:

    Login
    Search
    Cart
    Payment


Team capacity allows:

    Login
    Search
    Cart


These become part of the Sprint.


QA involvement:

    - Understand requirements
    - Review acceptance criteria
    - Identify test scenarios
    - Estimate testing effort
    - Identify dependencies
    - Identify risks
    - Identify required test data/environment


====================================================================
21. SPRINT REVIEW
====================================================================

Sprint Review occurs at the end of a Sprint.

The team demonstrates the completed product increment
to relevant stakeholders.

Example:

The team demonstrates:

    Login
    Search
    Cart


Stakeholders provide feedback.

That feedback can influence future backlog items.


QA role:

    - Ensure completed functionality is tested
    - Report quality status
    - Explain important defects
    - Support demonstration
    - Validate acceptance criteria


====================================================================
22. SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE
====================================================================

Retrospective is a meeting where the team reflects on the Sprint.

Typical questions:

    1. What went well?
    2. What did not go well?
    3. What should we improve?


Example:

Problem:

    QA received builds very late.

Improvement:

    Development and QA agree to provide testable builds earlier.


Another problem:

    Many defects were discovered late.

Improvement:

    QA participates earlier in requirement discussions.


Purpose:

    CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

25. QA DURING REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS
====================================================================

QA should understand:

    - What is being built?
    - Who will use it?
    - What are the business rules?
    - What are the acceptance criteria?
    - What can go wrong?
    - What are the edge cases?
    - What dependencies exist?


Example:

Requirement:

    "User can transfer money."


QA questions:

    - What is the minimum amount?
    - What is the maximum amount?
    - What happens with insufficient balance?
    - What happens with invalid account number?
    - What happens if network fails?
    - Is OTP required?
    - What happens if OTP expires?
    - What happens if transaction times out?


This is called early quality involvement.

31. DEFINITION OF DONE — BASIC CONCEPT
====================================================================

Definition of Done (DoD) represents the team's agreed conditions
for considering work complete.

Example:

A login story may be considered complete when:

    - Code is implemented.
    - Code review is completed.
    - Unit tests pass.
    - QA testing is completed.
    - Acceptance criteria are satisfied.
    - Critical defects are resolved.
    - Required automation is updated.
    - Documentation is updated where required.


The exact Definition of Done depends on the team's agreement.


====================================================================
32. DEFINITION OF READY — BASIC CONCEPT
====================================================================

Definition of Ready represents conditions indicating that a backlog
item is sufficiently prepared for development.

Example:

A story may be considered ready when:

    - Requirement is understood.
    - Acceptance criteria are available.
    - Dependencies are identified.
    - Required information is available.
    - Story is sufficiently clear for the team.


Important:

Definition of Ready:
    Ready to start.

Definition of Done:
    Complete according to team agreement.


====================================================================
33. AGILE TESTING PYRAMID — BASIC IDEA
====================================================================

A common testing strategy is to have more tests at lower levels
and fewer expensive end-to-end tests.

Conceptually:

             /\
            /  \
           / UI \
          /------\
         / API    \
        /----------\
       / Unit Tests \
      /______________\


Lower-level tests:

    Faster
    More numerous
    Usually easier to maintain


Higher-level UI tests:

    Slower
    More expensive
    Can be more fragile


This supports efficient continuous testing.

35. AGILE BUG HANDLING
====================================================================

Suppose QA finds a login defect.

Example:

Expected:

    Valid credentials should log the user in.


Actual:

    Application displays "Invalid password" even when the password
    is correct.


QA:

    1. Reproduces the defect.
    2. Records evidence.
    3. Creates bug in Jira.
    4. Assigns appropriate severity/priority.
    5. Developer investigates.
    6. Developer fixes the issue.
    7. QA retests.
    8. QA performs relevant regression testing.
    9. Bug is closed if successful.

37. EPIC, USER STORY, TASK, BUG
====================================================================

EPIC:

    Large feature or business capability.

Example:

    E-Commerce Checkout


USER STORY:

    Smaller user-focused requirement.

Example:

    "As a customer, I want to pay using UPI."


TASK:

    Technical or implementation work.

Example:

    "Integrate payment gateway API."


BUG:

    Problem where actual behavior differs from expected behavior.

Example:

    "UPI payment succeeds but order status is not updated."


Relationship:

    EPIC
      ↓
    USER STORIES
      ↓
    TASKS
      ↓
    DEVELOPMENT + TESTING

40. COMMON AGILE TESTING CHALLENGES
====================================================================

Challenge 1:
Frequent requirement changes

Solution:

    Maintain communication and update tests appropriately.


Challenge 2:
Short Sprints

Solution:

    Prioritize testing and automate repetitive regression tests.


Challenge 3:
Late builds

Solution:

    Improve development-QA collaboration and CI/CD.


Challenge 4:
Insufficient test data

Solution:

    Prepare test data requirements early.


Challenge 5:
Large regression suite

Solution:

    Automation + risk-based prioritization.


Challenge 6:
Unclear requirements

Solution:

    Discuss acceptance criteria before development.


Challenge 7:
Environment instability

Solution:

    Coordinate with development/DevOps and improve environment
    management.

====================================================================
42. AGILE VS WATERFALL INTERVIEW COMPARISON
====================================================================

Question:

"What is the main difference between Agile and Waterfall?"


Answer:

Waterfall follows a sequential development approach where phases
are generally completed in order, while Agile uses iterative and
incremental development with frequent feedback and the ability to
adapt to changing requirements.


Question:

"How is testing different in Agile?"

Answer:

Testing is continuous and integrated throughout the development
process. QA collaborates with developers and business stakeholders
from requirement analysis through delivery and improvement.

46. REAL-WORLD QA EXAMPLE
====================================================================

Project:

    Online Banking Application


User Story:

    "As a customer, I want to transfer money to another account."


Acceptance Criteria:

    1. User must be authenticated.
    2. Source account must be valid.
    3. Destination account must be valid.
    4. Amount must be greater than zero.
    5. User must have sufficient balance.
    6. Successful transfer should update the balance.
    7. Transaction should be recorded.
    8. Appropriate error should be displayed on failure.


QA Test Scenarios:

    - Valid transfer
    - Zero amount
    - Negative amount
    - Insufficient balance
    - Invalid destination
    - Same source and destination
    - Network failure
    - Session expiration
    - Duplicate transaction
    - Maximum transfer limit


Automation candidates:

    - API tests
    - Regression tests
    - Repetitive UI tests
    - Data validation


This demonstrates how Agile requirements become QA activities.

48. IMPORTANT INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
====================================================================

Q1. What is Agile?

Answer:

Agile is an iterative and incremental approach to software
development that emphasizes working software, collaboration,
customer feedback, and responding to change.


Q2. What is Scrum?

Answer:

Scrum is an Agile framework that organizes product development
into short iterations called Sprints.


Q3. What is a Sprint?

Answer:

A Sprint is a short, fixed development cycle during which the team
works on selected backlog items and aims to deliver a usable
product increment.


Q4. What is a Product Backlog?

Answer:

It is a prioritized list of work required for the product,
including features, bugs, improvements, and other work.


Q5. What is a Sprint Backlog?

Answer:

It is the work selected from the Product Backlog for the current
Sprint.


Q6. What is a User Story?

Answer:

A User Story describes a requirement from the perspective of a
user, commonly written as:

"As a user, I want X so that Y."


Q7. What are Acceptance Criteria?

Answer:

Acceptance Criteria define the conditions that a user story must
satisfy to be considered acceptable.


Q8. What is the role of a Product Owner?

Answer:

The Product Owner represents product/business needs, manages and
prioritizes the Product Backlog, and helps clarify requirements.


Q9. What is the role of a Scrum Master?

Answer:

The Scrum Master facilitates Scrum, supports the team, helps remove
impediments, and promotes effective Scrum practices.


Q10. What is Daily Stand-up?

Answer:

It is a short daily synchronization meeting where team members
discuss progress, upcoming work, and blockers.


Q11. What happens in Sprint Planning?

Answer:

The team determines the Sprint goal and selects/organizes work
from the Product Backlog for the Sprint.


Q12. What is Sprint Review?

Answer:

It is a session at the end of the Sprint where the team reviews or
demonstrates the completed product increment and receives feedback.


Q13. What is Sprint Retrospective?

Answer:

It is a meeting where the team reflects on the Sprint and identifies
ways to improve its process and collaboration.


Q14. What is the role of QA in Agile?

Answer:

QA participates throughout the Sprint, including requirement
analysis, test design, continuous testing, defect reporting,
retesting, regression testing, automation, review, and continuous
improvement.


Q15. Is testing performed only at the end of an Agile Sprint?

Answer:

No. Testing is integrated throughout development and is performed
continuously.


Q16. Does Agile mean there is no documentation?

Answer:

No. Agile values working software more highly than comprehensive
documentation, but useful documentation is still created when
needed.


Q17. How does QA handle changing requirements in Agile?

Answer:

QA collaborates closely with the team, updates test scenarios and
cases, analyzes impact, prioritizes testing, and adapts to the
updated acceptance criteria.


Q18. What is Definition of Done?

Answer:

It is the team's agreed set of conditions that must be satisfied
for work to be considered complete.


Q19. What is the difference between Definition of Ready and
Definition of Done?

Answer:

Definition of Ready indicates that a backlog item is sufficiently
prepared to start, while Definition of Done indicates that the work
meets the team's agreed completion criteria.


Q20. Why is automation important in Agile?

Answer:

Frequent development cycles create frequent regression needs.
Automation provides faster and repeatable feedback and helps the
team maintain quality across iterations.

49. MOST IMPORTANT INTERVIEW DIFFERENCES
====================================================================

PRODUCT BACKLOG vs SPRINT BACKLOG

Product Backlog:
    Entire prioritized product work.

Sprint Backlog:
    Work selected for current Sprint.


SPRINT REVIEW vs RETROSPECTIVE

Sprint Review:
    Focuses on the product/increment and stakeholder feedback.

Retrospective:
    Focuses on team/process improvement.


PRODUCT OWNER vs SCRUM MASTER

Product Owner:
    Product value, requirements, backlog prioritization.

Scrum Master:
    Scrum facilitation, team support, impediment removal.


USER STORY vs ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

User Story:
    Describes what the user wants and why.

Acceptance Criteria:
    Defines conditions that must be satisfied.


AGILE vs SCRUM

Agile:
    Broader development philosophy/approach based on Agile values
    and principles.

Scrum:
    A specific framework used to implement an Agile way of working.
