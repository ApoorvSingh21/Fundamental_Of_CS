CHAPTER 18 — SEVERITY & PRIORITY
====================================================================

GOAL
----
Understand how QA Engineers classify defects based on:

    1. Severity
    2. Priority

You will learn:

    - Severity definition
    - Priority definition
    - Severity levels
    - Priority levels
    - Severity vs Priority
    - Critical/High/Medium/Low examples
    - Different Severity/Priority combinations
    - Practical QA scenarios
    - Interview questions
    - Scenario-based questions


====================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
====================================================================

When a tester finds a defect, simply reporting "there is a bug" is
not enough.

The team also needs to understand:

    How badly does this defect affect the system?

and

    How urgently should this defect be fixed?

These questions lead to:

    SEVERITY
        and
    PRIORITY


====================================================================
2. WHAT IS SEVERITY?
====================================================================

Severity describes the impact or seriousness of a defect on the
application.

In simple words:

    Severity = How badly does the defect affect the system?


Example:

A payment system crashes whenever a customer tries to pay.

Impact:

    Very high

Therefore:

    Severity = Critical/High depending on the project's defined
               severity scale.


====================================================================
3. WHAT IS PRIORITY?
====================================================================

Priority describes how urgently a defect should be addressed or
fixed.

In simple words:

    Priority = How urgently should we fix this defect?


Example:

A spelling mistake appears on the homepage immediately before a
major public release.

Technical impact:

    Low

But business visibility:

    High

Therefore:

    Severity = Low
    Priority = High


====================================================================
4. SIMPLE DIFFERENCE
====================================================================

SEVERITY:

    Impact of the defect

PRIORITY:

    Urgency of fixing the defect


Think:

    Severity → "How bad is it?"

    Priority → "How soon should we fix it?"


====================================================================
5. SEVERITY LEVELS
====================================================================

The syllabus defines the common levels as:

    Critical
    High
    Medium
    Low


Important:

Severity definitions can vary between organizations, but the
general meaning is based on impact.


====================================================================
6. CRITICAL SEVERITY
====================================================================

Critical severity indicates an extremely serious defect that can
severely affect an essential system function.

Examples:

    - Application cannot start
    - Critical business functionality completely unavailable
    - Major transaction functionality fails
    - Severe system failure


Example:

Banking application:

    Customer cannot perform any transaction because the transaction
    system is unavailable.

Potential classification:

    Severity = Critical


====================================================================
7. HIGH SEVERITY
====================================================================

High severity indicates a major functional problem that significantly
affects important application functionality.

Examples:

    - Checkout does not work
    - Important business function fails
    - Major calculation is incorrect
    - Important user workflow cannot be completed


Example:

E-commerce:

    User can browse products and add items to cart, but checkout
    cannot be completed.

Possible classification:

    Severity = High


====================================================================
8. MEDIUM SEVERITY
====================================================================

Medium severity indicates a defect with noticeable impact but without
completely blocking a critical application function.

Examples:

    - Important but non-critical feature behaves incorrectly
    - Some workflow is affected
    - Incorrect validation in a non-critical area


Example:

    Search filtering produces incorrect results for one condition,
    while normal product browsing continues to work.


====================================================================
9. LOW SEVERITY
====================================================================

Low severity indicates a defect with relatively small impact.

Examples:

    - Minor UI issue
    - Small alignment problem
    - Typographical error
    - Minor visual inconsistency


Example:

    A button is slightly misaligned but remains completely usable.

Possible classification:

    Severity = Low


====================================================================
10. PRIORITY LEVELS
====================================================================

Priority is used to determine the urgency of fixing a defect.

Common classifications include:

    High
    Medium
    Low


--------------------------------------------------------------------
HIGH PRIORITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The defect should be addressed urgently.

Examples:

    - Major customer-facing issue
    - Critical business workflow problem
    - Release-blocking issue
    - Highly visible production problem


--------------------------------------------------------------------
MEDIUM PRIORITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The defect should be fixed, but it may not require immediate action.

Examples:

    - Important functionality issue
    - Noticeable issue with a workaround
    - Defect planned for the current release


--------------------------------------------------------------------
LOW PRIORITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The defect can generally be addressed later.

Examples:

    - Minor UI issue
    - Typographical error in a less important area
    - Small visual inconsistency


====================================================================
11. SEVERITY vs PRIORITY
====================================================================

+----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| SEVERITY       | PRIORITY                                    |
+----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Measures       | Measures urgency                            |
| impact         |                                             |
+----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Technical/     | Business/release importance often affects   |
| functional     | urgency                                     |
| impact         |                                             |
+----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| How bad is it? | How soon should it be fixed?                |
+----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Focuses on     | Focuses on fixing order                     |
| defect impact  |                                             |
+----------------+---------------------------------------------+


Simple memory trick:

    S = Severity = Seriousness

    P = Priority = Preference/order of fixing


====================================================================
12. IMPORTANT: SEVERITY AND PRIORITY ARE NOT THE SAME
====================================================================

A common beginner mistake is:

    High Severity = High Priority
    Low Severity  = Low Priority

This is NOT always true.

Possible combinations include:

    1. High Severity + High Priority
    2. High Severity + Low Priority
    3. Low Severity + High Priority
    4. Low Severity + Low Priority


====================================================================
13. HIGH SEVERITY + HIGH PRIORITY
====================================================================

Meaning:

    The defect has major impact AND needs urgent attention.


Example:

    E-commerce payment is completely unavailable.

Impact:

    Customers cannot complete purchases.

Classification:

    Severity = High/Critical
    Priority = High


Another example:

    Production login is unavailable for all users.

Impact:

    Major application functionality is blocked.

Priority:

    Immediate attention required.


====================================================================
14. HIGH SEVERITY + LOW PRIORITY
====================================================================

This combination can occur when a defect has serious technical or
functional impact but affects a low-use or obsolete area, or when
business decisions postpone the fix.

Example:

    A severe issue exists in an old feature that is rarely used and
    is scheduled for retirement.

Possible classification:

    Severity = High
    Priority = Low


Important:

The exact classification depends on the application's context and
team decisions.


====================================================================
15. LOW SEVERITY + HIGH PRIORITY
====================================================================

This is one of the most common interview examples.

Example:

    Company name is misspelled on the main public homepage.

Technical impact:

    Low

Visibility:

    Very high

Business/reputation importance:

    High

Therefore:

    Severity = Low
    Priority = High


Another example:

    A major promotional banner contains an incorrect price.

The UI problem may appear simple, but the business impact may make it
urgent.


====================================================================
16. LOW SEVERITY + LOW PRIORITY
====================================================================

Meaning:

    The defect has low impact and does not need urgent attention.


Example:

    Small alignment issue on an internal informational page.

Classification:

    Severity = Low
    Priority = Low

====================================================================
22. SEVERITY DECISION QUESTIONS
====================================================================

When determining severity, ask:

    1. What functionality is affected?
    2. Is a critical workflow blocked?
    3. Is data affected?
    4. Is the system unavailable?
    5. Is the business function significantly affected?
    6. Is there a workaround?
    7. How broadly does the issue affect users?


The exact severity should follow the organization's defined
classification rules.


====================================================================
23. PRIORITY DECISION QUESTIONS
====================================================================

When determining priority, ask:

    1. Is the issue release-blocking?
    2. Is it customer-facing?
    3. How many users are affected?
    4. Is it business-critical?
    5. Is there a deadline?
    6. Is the issue highly visible?
    7. Is there a workaround?
    8. Is the affected feature currently important?


Priority should be aligned with business and release needs.


====================================================================
24. ROLE OF QA IN SEVERITY AND PRIORITY
====================================================================

QA should provide accurate information about:

    - Impact
    - Reproducibility
    - Affected functionality
    - Environment
    - User impact
    - Business behavior

QA may suggest severity and priority according to the team's process.

However, final classification can involve:

    QA
    Development
    Product Owner
    Business stakeholders
    Project/Release management


The exact process varies by organization.

27. TRICKY INTERVIEW EXAMPLE
====================================================================

Question:

    A spelling mistake exists on the homepage.
    What are the severity and priority?

Possible answer:

    Severity = Low
    Priority = High

Why?

    The typo has low functional/technical impact, but because the
    homepage is highly visible, the business may want it fixed
    immediately.


Important:

    Do not memorize this as an absolute rule.

The exact classification depends on the context and project policy.


====================================================================
28. ANOTHER TRICKY EXAMPLE
====================================================================

Question:

    A severe defect exists in a feature that is scheduled for
    complete removal in the next release.

Possible:

    Severity = High
    Priority = Low

Why?

    The technical impact may be serious, but fixing the feature may
    not be worth the effort if it is about to be removed.


Again:

    Business context matters.

====================================================================
31. COMMON MISTAKES
====================================================================

MISTAKE 1:

    "All High Severity bugs must have High Priority."

Incorrect.

Severity and priority measure different things.


MISTAKE 2:

    "Tester decides priority alone."

Not necessarily.

Priority can involve product/business/release decisions.


MISTAKE 3:

    "Low severity means unimportant."

Not always.

A low-severity issue can have high business visibility and therefore
high priority.


MISTAKE 4:

    "Severity is based only on how annoying the bug is."

Incorrect.

Severity is based on impact.


MISTAKE 5:

    Ignoring business context.

Example:

    Small UI problem on a major public campaign page may become highly
    urgent.

33. PRACTICAL QA DECISION PROCESS
====================================================================

When a defect is found:

STEP 1:
    Understand the affected functionality.

STEP 2:
    Determine impact.

STEP 3:
    Ask whether critical functionality is blocked.

STEP 4:
    Determine severity according to project rules.

STEP 5:
    Determine business/release urgency.

STEP 6:
    Assign or suggest priority according to team process.

STEP 7:
    Document the reasoning when necessary.

39. INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
====================================================================

Q1. What is severity?

Answer:

Severity represents the impact or seriousness of a defect on the
application.


Q2. What is priority?

Answer:

Priority represents how urgently a defect should be addressed or
fixed.


Q3. What is the difference between severity and priority?

Answer:

Severity focuses on impact, while priority focuses on urgency.


Q4. Can a low-severity defect have high priority?

Answer:

Yes.

Example:

A minor typo on a highly visible public page before a major release.


Q5. Can a high-severity defect have low priority?

Answer:

Yes, depending on business context.

For example, a severe issue in a feature that is about to be retired
may be given low priority.


Q6. Who decides priority?

Answer:

It depends on the organization. QA may suggest it, but Product,
business, development, release management, or other stakeholders may
also participate.


Q7. What is critical severity?

Answer:

It represents extremely serious impact on an essential system
function.


Q8. What is low severity?

Answer:

It represents relatively small impact, such as a minor UI or
typographical issue.

42. IMPORTANT INTERVIEW RULE
====================================================================

Do NOT answer severity/priority questions only by memorizing a table.

Instead explain your reasoning.

For example:

     and must be corrected before the release."
