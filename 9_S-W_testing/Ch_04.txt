OBJECTIVE
---------
Understand the major Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) models
and how software development and testing activities are organized
in each model.

This chapter covers:

    1. Waterfall Model
    2. V-Model
    3. Iterative Model
    4. Incremental Model
    5. Spiral Model
    6. Prototype Model
    7. Agile Model

IMPORTANT CONCEPT
-----------------
SDLC describes the overall software development life cycle.

An SDLC MODEL describes how the activities of the life cycle are
organized and performed.


====================================================================
1. WHAT IS AN SDLC MODEL?
====================================================================

An SDLC model is a structured approach used to organize software
development activities.

Different projects have different:

    - Requirements
    - Risks
    - Deadlines
    - Customer needs
    - Complexity
    - Change frequency
    - Quality requirements

Therefore, organizations can use different models depending on the
project.


The major models in this chapter are:

    V-Model

       v
    Iterative
       |
       v

       v
    Spiral
       |
       v
    Prototype
       |
       v
    Agile


====================================================================
2. WATERFALL MODEL
====================================================================

The Waterfall Model is a sequential development model.

In a simplified Waterfall process, one major phase is completed
before moving to the next phase.

Basic flow:

    Requirements
         |
         v
       Design
         |
         v
     Development
         |
         v
       Testing
         |
         v
     Deployment
         |
         v
     Maintenance


The flow is primarily sequential.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 WATERFALL PHASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------

PHASE 1 — REQUIREMENTS
----------------------
Requirements are collected and analyzed.

Example:

    Customer wants:

        - Login
        - Product search
        - Shopping cart
        - Checkout


PHASE 2 — DESIGN
----------------
The team designs the solution.

May include:

    - Architecture
    - UI
    - Database
    - Components


PHASE 3 — DEVELOPMENT
---------------------
Developers implement the designed system.


PHASE 4 — TESTING
-----------------
The implemented software is tested.


PHASE 5 — DEPLOYMENT
--------------------
The completed software is released.


PHASE 6 — MAINTENANCE
---------------------
The software is maintained after release.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 WATERFALL EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose a project follows:

    Requirements
          ↓
        Design
          ↓
      Development
          ↓
        Testing
          ↓
      Deployment


The team generally progresses through these stages in sequence.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 ADVANTAGES OF WATERFALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages include:

    - Simple and easy-to-understand structure
    - Clearly defined phases
    - Documentation can be planned around phases
    - Easier to track phase completion


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 LIMITATIONS OF WATERFALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential limitations include:

    - Changes can be difficult after a phase is completed.
    - Customer feedback may arrive relatively late.
    - Working software may be available late.
    - Defects discovered late can require significant rework.
    - It is less suitable when requirements change frequently.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 WHEN WATERFALL MAY FIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------

It may be more appropriate when:

    - Requirements are relatively stable.
    - The project is well understood.
    - Changes are expected to be limited.
    - A sequential process is suitable.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 QA ROLE IN WATERFALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

QA/Test activities are strongly associated with the testing phase,
but quality-related work can also involve understanding
requirements and preparing tests before execution.

Typical QA activities include:

    - Requirement review
    - Test planning
    - Test case preparation
    - Test execution
    - Defect reporting
    - Retesting
    - Regression testing


====================================================================
3. V-MODEL
====================================================================

V-Model is often described as an extension or refinement of the
sequential approach where development activities are paired with
corresponding testing activities.

The "V" represents the relationship between development and testing
activities.


Simplified representation:

             REQUIREMENTS
                  |
                  |
             SYSTEM DESIGN
                  |
                  |
          ARCHITECTURE DESIGN
                  |
                  |
           DETAILED DESIGN
                  |
                  |
             DEVELOPMENT
                  |
                  |
                  v
             UNIT TESTING
                  |
                  v
          INTEGRATION TESTING
                  |
                  v
            SYSTEM TESTING
                  |
                  v
        ACCEPTANCE TESTING


The left side focuses on development/specification activities.

The right side focuses on corresponding testing activities.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 V-MODEL CONCEPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A major idea of the V-Model is that testing activities are planned
alongside corresponding development activities rather than waiting
until development is completely finished.


Conceptually:

    REQUIREMENTS  ----------------> ACCEPTANCE TESTING

    SYSTEM DESIGN ----------------> SYSTEM TESTING

    ARCHITECTURE -----------------> INTEGRATION TESTING

    DETAILED DESIGN --------------> UNIT TESTING

                    DEVELOPMENT
                         |
                         v


This helps establish a relationship between what is specified and
how it will be tested.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 V-MODEL AND QA
--------------------------------------------------------------------

QA can become involved early.

Examples:

    Requirement stage
        |
        +---- Acceptance test planning

    System design
        |
        +---- System test planning

    Architecture
        |
        +---- Integration test planning

    Detailed design
        |
        +---- Unit-level test considerations


The important concept is:

    TEST PLANNING STARTS EARLY.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 ADVANTAGES OF V-MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages:

    - Testing is considered early.
    - Clear relationship between development and testing.
    - Structured process.
    - Test planning can begin early.
    - Can work well when requirements are stable.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 LIMITATIONS OF V-MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential limitations:

    - Changes can be difficult.
    - Less flexible for frequently changing requirements.
    - Requires relatively clear requirements.
    - Sequential nature can make rapid changes difficult.


====================================================================
4. ITERATIVE MODEL
====================================================================

The Iterative Model develops software through repeated cycles.

Instead of trying to complete everything in one large cycle, the
team repeatedly develops and improves the system.


Basic concept:

             ITERATION 1
                 |
                 v
             Initial Version
                 |
                 v
             ITERATION 2
                 |
                 v
            Improved Version
                 |
                 v
             ITERATION 3
                 |
                 v
           Further Improvement


Each iteration can involve activities such as:

    - Planning
    - Requirements refinement
    - Design
    - Development
    - Testing
    - Feedback
    - Improvement


--------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 ITERATIVE EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose an application has ten major features.

Instead of attempting a single large cycle:

    All requirements
          |
          v
    Complete application


The team may work repeatedly:

    Iteration 1
        |
        +---- Basic login
        |
        v
    Iteration 2
        |
        +---- Improve login
        +---- Add profile
        |
        v
    Iteration 3
        |
        +---- Add search
        +---- Improve existing features
        |
        v
    Iteration 4
        |
        +---- Add checkout


The exact planning depends on the project.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 ITERATIVE TESTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Testing occurs repeatedly.

Example:

    Iteration 1
        |
        v
      Testing
        |
        v
      Feedback
        |
        v
    Iteration 2
        |
        v
      Testing
        |
        v
      Feedback


QA can test new functionality and the impact of changes from
previous iterations.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 ADVANTAGES OF ITERATIVE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages:

    - Frequent improvement
    - Early versions can be evaluated
    - Feedback can influence later iterations
    - Risks can be identified earlier than in a purely sequential
      approach


--------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 LIMITATIONS OF ITERATIVE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential limitations:

    - Requires careful planning of iterations.
    - Repeated changes can increase complexity.
    - Architecture may need to evolve as requirements become clearer.


====================================================================
5. INCREMENTAL MODEL
====================================================================

The Incremental Model develops the system in multiple increments.

Each increment delivers a portion of the overall functionality.


Basic concept:

    Complete Product
          |
          |
          +-----------------------------+
          |                             |
          v                             v
      Increment 1                  Increment 2
          |                             |
          v                             v
      Feature Set 1                Feature Set 2
          |
          v
      Increment 3
          |
          v
      Feature Set 3
          |
          v
     Complete System


--------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 INCREMENT EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose an e-commerce application requires:

    1. Registration
    2. Login
    3. Product Search
    4. Cart
    5. Checkout
    6. Payment


The team could deliver:

    Increment 1:
        Registration + Login

    Increment 2:
        Product Search

    Increment 3:
        Cart

    Increment 4:
        Checkout

    Increment 5:
        Payment


Each increment adds functionality to the product.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 INCREMENTAL TESTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Each increment needs appropriate testing.

Example:

    Increment 1
        |
        v
    Test Increment 1
        |
        v
    Increment 2
        |
        v
    Test New Functionality
        |
        v
    Regression Testing
        |
        v
    Increment 3
        |
        v
    More Testing


As new functionality is added, existing functionality may also
need regression testing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 ADVANTAGES OF INCREMENTAL MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages:

    - Functionality can be delivered in parts.
    - Users may receive useful functionality earlier.
    - Feedback can influence later increments.
    - Large systems can be divided into manageable pieces.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4 LIMITATIONS OF INCREMENTAL MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential limitations:

    - Requires good planning of increments.
    - Integration between increments can create complexity.
    - Dependencies between features need careful management.


====================================================================
6. ITERATIVE vs INCREMENTAL
====================================================================

These two concepts are related but should not be treated as
identical.


ITERATIVE
---------

Main idea:

    Repeatedly improve/refine the system.

Example:

    Version 1
       |
       v
    Improve
       |
       v
    Version 2
       |
       v
    Improve
       |
       v
    Version 3


INCREMENTAL
-----------

Main idea:

    Add new portions of functionality.

Example:

    Base Product
       |
       +---- Feature A
       |
       +---- Feature B
       |
       +---- Feature C


Simple memory trick:

    ITERATIVE
        = Improve repeatedly

    INCREMENTAL
        = Add functionality in pieces


A real project can combine both approaches.


====================================================================
7. SPIRAL MODEL
====================================================================

The Spiral Model is a risk-oriented development model.

It combines repeated development cycles with explicit attention
to risk analysis.

The project progresses through spiral cycles.


Simplified concept:

             +--------------------+
             | Planning           |
             +--------------------+
                      |
                      v
             +--------------------+
             | Risk Analysis      |
             +--------------------+
                      |
                      v
             +--------------------+
             | Development/Test   |
             +--------------------+
                      |
                      v
             +--------------------+
             | Evaluation         |
             +--------------------+
                      |
                      v
                  Next Cycle
                      |
                      v
                  Next Spiral


The exact structure can vary, but risk analysis is a central idea.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 SPIRAL MODEL AND RISK
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Risk means the possibility of an undesirable outcome.

Examples:

    - Technical risk
    - Security risk
    - Performance risk
    - Requirement risk
    - Integration risk


The Spiral Model explicitly emphasizes identifying and addressing
important risks during development.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
7.2 SPIRAL EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose a company is building a complex banking system.

Potential risks:

    - Security
    - Data integrity
    - Transaction reliability
    - Performance
    - Integration with external systems


The team can:

    1. Plan
    2. Identify risks
    3. Analyze/mitigate risks
    4. Develop/test
    5. Evaluate
    6. Start the next cycle


--------------------------------------------------------------------
7.3 ADVANTAGES OF SPIRAL MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages:

    - Strong focus on risk management
    - Useful for complex/high-risk projects
    - Feedback can be incorporated through cycles
    - Risks can be addressed throughout development


--------------------------------------------------------------------
7.4 LIMITATIONS OF SPIRAL MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential limitations:

    - Can be complex to manage.
    - Risk analysis requires expertise.
    - May require more resources.
    - Can be unsuitable for small/simple projects where the
      overhead is unnecessary.


====================================================================
8. PROTOTYPE MODEL
====================================================================

The Prototype Model creates an early version or prototype of a
system or feature to help clarify requirements and expectations.


Basic flow:

    Initial Requirements
           |
           v
        Prototype
           |
           v
        Feedback
           |
           v
    Refined Requirements
           |
           v
       Development


A prototype is not necessarily the final production system.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 WHY USE A PROTOTYPE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A prototype can help stakeholders visualize and understand the
proposed system.

This is especially useful when requirements are unclear or when
the user interface or behavior needs clarification.


Example:

Customer says:

    "I want a simple banking dashboard."


This is vague.

The team creates a prototype:

    +----------------------------------+
    |          BANKING DASHBOARD       |
    +----------------------------------+
    | Balance: ₹50,000                 |
    |                                  |
    | [Transfer] [Deposit] [History]   |
    +----------------------------------+


Customer reviews it and says:

    "Add transaction search."

The team can refine the requirements based on this feedback.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 PROTOTYPE TYPES — GENERAL IDEA
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A prototype can vary in fidelity.

It may be:

    - Simple sketches
    - Wireframes
    - Interactive UI mockups
    - More functional experimental software


The appropriate type depends on what needs to be learned.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
8.3 ADVANTAGES OF PROTOTYPE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages:

    - Helps clarify unclear requirements.
    - Provides early user feedback.
    - Helps visualize the proposed solution.
    - Can identify misunderstandings early.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
8.4 LIMITATIONS OF PROTOTYPE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential limitations:

    - Users may mistake the prototype for the final product.
    - Frequent changes can increase effort.
    - Prototype code may not be suitable for production.
    - Poorly managed prototypes can create unrealistic
      expectations.


====================================================================
9. AGILE MODEL
====================================================================

Agile is an approach that emphasizes:

    - Frequent delivery
    - Collaboration
    - Customer/user feedback
    - Adaptability to change
    - Iterative and incremental development


Instead of waiting for one large final release, Agile teams work
in smaller cycles and deliver functionality frequently.


Simplified flow:

    Product Backlog
          |
          v
        Sprint
          |
          v
    Development
          |
          v
       Testing
          |
          v
        Review
          |
          v
     Retrospective
          |
          v
     Next Sprint


Agile is covered in much greater detail in Chapter 5.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 AGILE ITERATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

An Agile team commonly works in short development cycles called
sprints in Scrum.

A simplified cycle:

    Sprint Planning
          |
          v
       Development
          |
          v
        Testing
          |
          v
     Sprint Review
          |
          v
    Retrospective
          |
          v
      Next Sprint


The exact process depends on the Agile framework and organization.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
9.2 AGILE AND QA
--------------------------------------------------------------------

In Agile, QA is generally integrated into the development process
rather than treated as an isolated final-stage activity.

QA can participate in:

    - Requirement discussions
    - User story analysis
    - Acceptance criteria review
    - Test design
    - Test execution
    - Defect reporting
    - Regression testing
    - Sprint activities


Example:

    User Story
         |
         v
    Acceptance Criteria
         |
         v
    Development
         |
         v
       Testing
         |
         v
    Feedback
         |
         v
    Next Sprint


--------------------------------------------------------------------
9.3 ADVANTAGES OF AGILE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential advantages:

    - Adapts to changing requirements.
    - Frequent feedback.
    - Frequent delivery of functionality.
    - Close collaboration.
    - Problems can be discovered earlier through repeated cycles.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
9.4 LIMITATIONS / CHALLENGES OF AGILE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Potential challenges:

    - Requires strong collaboration.
    - Frequent changes require good communication.
    - Scope can evolve continuously.
    - Teams need discipline and effective prioritization.
    - Poorly implemented Agile can create confusion.

====================================================================

Both involve repeated cycles.

ITERATIVE:

    Main focus
        =
    Repeated improvement/refinement


SPIRAL:

    Main focus
        =
    Repeated cycles + explicit risk analysis


Memory:

    ITERATIVE = Repeat and improve

    SPIRAL = Repeat + analyze/manage risks


====================================================================
16. PROTOTYPE vs FINAL PRODUCT
====================================================================

PROTOTYPE
---------

Purpose:

    Learn, explore, clarify, and collect feedback.


FINAL PRODUCT
-------------

Purpose:

    Provide the complete production functionality required by the
    project.


Example:

    Prototype:
        Login screen demonstration

    Final product:
        Fully functional login system with backend, database,
        authentication, error handling, security, etc.

20. WHICH MODEL SHOULD A PROJECT USE?
====================================================================

There is no single model that is best for every project.

The choice depends on factors such as:

    - Requirement stability
    - Project complexity
    - Risk
    - Customer involvement
    - Expected changes
    - Delivery requirements
    - Team capabilities
    - Project constraints


General understanding:

    Stable, well-understood requirements
            |
            v
       Sequential model
       may be suitable


    Frequently changing requirements
            |
            v
       Agile/adaptive
       approach may be suitable


    High-risk/complex project
            |
            v
       Risk-focused approach
       such as Spiral may be considered


    Unclear requirements
            |
            v
       Prototype approach
       may help clarify needs


These are general guidelines; actual model selection depends on
the project and organization.

21. IMPORTANT INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
====================================================================

Q1. What is an SDLC model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
An SDLC model is a structured approach for organizing and
performing software development life-cycle activities.


Q2. What is the Waterfall Model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Waterfall is a sequential development model in which major phases
are generally performed one after another.


Q3. What is the V-Model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The V-Model is a structured model that associates development/
specification activities with corresponding testing activities and
emphasizes early test planning.


Q4. What is the Iterative Model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
It develops and improves software through repeated cycles.


Q5. What is the Incremental Model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
It delivers the system through multiple increments, with each
increment adding functionality.


Q6. What is the Spiral Model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
It is a risk-oriented model that uses repeated cycles with
explicit risk analysis and evaluation.


Q7. What is the Prototype Model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
It creates an early representation of a system or feature to help
clarify requirements and obtain feedback.


Q8. What is Agile?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Agile is an adaptive approach emphasizing iterative/incremental
development, collaboration, frequent feedback, and responsiveness
to change.


Q9. What is the main difference between Waterfall and Agile?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Waterfall is primarily sequential, while Agile commonly uses
iterative and incremental development with frequent feedback and
adaptation.


Q10. What is the main focus of Spiral?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Risk identification, analysis, and management are central to the
Spiral approach.


Q11. What is the difference between Iterative and Incremental?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Iterative development emphasizes repeated improvement/refinement,
while incremental development emphasizes adding functionality in
multiple pieces.


Q12. Why is the V-Model important for testers?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
It explicitly connects development/specification activities with
corresponding testing activities and encourages test planning
early.


Q13. When can a prototype be useful?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A prototype can be useful when requirements or the proposed
solution need clarification and stakeholder feedback.


Q14. Does Agile mean there is no documentation or planning?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The source syllabus does not support that claim. Agile is described
as emphasizing adaptability, collaboration, iterative/incremental
development, and frequent feedback.

24. CHAPTER 4 KEY TAKEAWAYS
====================================================================

1. SDLC models define how software-development activities are
   organized.

2. Waterfall follows a primarily sequential approach.

3. V-Model connects development/specification activities with
   corresponding testing activities.

4. Iterative development repeatedly improves the system.

5. Incremental development adds functionality in multiple pieces.

6. Spiral emphasizes risk analysis and management.

7. Prototype development helps clarify unclear requirements through
   early representations and feedback.

8. Agile emphasizes collaboration, frequent feedback,
   iterative/incremental development, and adaptation.

9. Testing activities differ depending on the selected model.

10. A QA Engineer should understand the SDLC model because it
    affects test planning, execution, feedback, regression,
    releases, and collaboration.

11. No single SDLC model is automatically best for every project.

12. The next chapter focuses specifically on Agile fundamentals.
