2. WHY DO WE TRANSFORM A GRAMMAR?
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A grammar may be correct in terms of the language it describes but
still be inconvenient or unsuitable for a particular parser.

For example:

    E → E + T | T


This grammar is commonly used to describe expressions.

But notice:

    E → E + T

The right side begins with E itself.

This is:

    LEFT RECURSION


A naive recursive-descent parser can get stuck in an infinite recursion.

Therefore we transform the grammar.


Basic idea:

    Original CFG
        ↓
    Grammar Transformation
        ↓
    Parser-friendly grammar
        ↓
    Predictive / LL parser


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
3. IMPORTANT NOTE — LANGUAGE PRESERVATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A grammar transformation is normally performed so that the transformed
grammar generates the same intended language.

Conceptually:

    Original Grammar G
           ↓
      Transformation
           ↓
    New Grammar G'


Ideally:

    L(G) = L(G')


However, some transformations may alter parse-tree structure or
associativity while preserving the terminal language.

Therefore always distinguish:

    Language equivalence

from:

    Parse-tree equivalence.


This distinction becomes important when removing left recursion from
expression grammars.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
4. LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A grammar is left-recursive if a non-terminal can derive a string whose
leftmost symbol is itself.

The simplest form is:

    A → Aα | β


Here:

    A

appears at the beginning of one of its own productions.


Example:

    E → E + T | T


The production:

    E → E + T

is immediately left-recursive.


Memory:

    A → A...

means:

    LEFT RECURSION


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
5. WHY LEFT RECURSION IS A PROBLEM FOR TOP-DOWN PARSERS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Consider:

    E → E + T | T


Suppose a recursive-descent parser has a function:

    parseE()


It may try:

    parseE()
       ↓
    E → E + T
       ↓
    parseE()
       ↓
    E → E + T
       ↓
    parseE()
       ↓
       ...


This can continue indefinitely.

Therefore:

    Left recursion is problematic for naive top-down recursive parsing.


Important:

    Left recursion is not inherently bad for ALL parsers.

Bottom-up parsers such as LR parsers can handle left-recursive grammars
very naturally.

This is a major exam/interview point.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
6. IMMEDIATE LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Immediate left recursion occurs when a production has the form:

    A → Aα


The same non-terminal A appears directly at the beginning of its
right-hand side.

General grammar:

    A → Aα₁ | Aα₂ | ... | β₁ | β₂ | ...


where:

    βᵢ does NOT begin with A.


This is called:

    IMMEDIATE LEFT RECURSION


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
7. SIMPLE EXAMPLE OF IMMEDIATE LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Grammar:

    A → Aα | β


Here:

    A → Aα

is immediately left-recursive.


The non-left-recursive alternative is:

    A → β


We want to transform the grammar.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
8. STANDARD LEFT-RECURSION REMOVAL FORMULA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Given:

    A → Aα₁ | Aα₂ | ... | β₁ | β₂ | ...


Transform into:

    A  → β₁A' | β₂A' | ...


    A' → α₁A' | α₂A' | ... | ε


This removes immediate left recursion.


This formula is extremely important for GATE.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
9. SIMPLE LEFT-RECURSION REMOVAL EXAMPLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    A → Aα | β


Apply the formula.

New grammar:

    A  → βA'

    A' → αA' | ε


The left recursion is removed.


Original:

    A → Aα | β


Transformed:

    A  → βA'
    A' → αA' | ε


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
10. WHY THE TRANSFORMATION WORKS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original grammar:

    A → Aα | β


Possible derivations:

    A
    ⇒ β

or:

    A
    ⇒ Aα
    ⇒ Aαα
    ⇒ Aααα
    ⇒ ...
    ⇒ βα
    ⇒ βαα
    ⇒ βααα
    ...


Therefore the language has the form:

    βαⁿ

for n ≥ 0.

The transformed grammar:

    A  → βA'
    A' → αA' | ε


can generate:

    β
    βα
    βαα
    βααα
    ...


So the same pattern is generated without left recursion.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
11. EXAMPLE — EXPRESSION GRAMMAR
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    E → E + T | T


Identify:

    α = +T

    β = T


Apply formula:

    E  → T E'

    E' → + T E' | ε


Therefore:

    Original:

        E → E + T | T


    Transformed:

        E  → T E'
        E' → + T E' | ε


Now E is not left-recursive.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
12. ANOTHER EXAMPLE — MULTIPLICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    T → T * F | F


Here:

    α = *F

    β = F


Transform:

    T  → F T'

    T' → * F T' | ε


Therefore:

    T → F T'
    T' → *FT' | ε


This is suitable for top-down parsing.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
13. COMPLETE EXPRESSION GRAMMAR TRANSFORMATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    E → E + T | T

    T → T * F | F

    F → (E) | id


The grammar contains left recursion in:

    E
    T


Remove it.

For E:

    E  → T E'
    E' → + T E' | ε


For T:

    T  → F T'
    T' → * F T' | ε


F remains:

    F → (E) | id


Final grammar:

    E  → T E'
    E' → + T E' | ε

    T  → F T'
    T' → * F T' | ε

    F  → (E) | id


This is a very important grammar transformation.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
14. WHAT CHANGED?
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    E → E + T | T

The structure naturally represented repeated addition as:

    ((id + id) + id)


The transformed grammar:

    E → T E'
    E' → + T E' | ε


represents the repeated additions through E'.

The terminal language is preserved.

But the parse-tree structure is different.

This is why:

    Language equivalence

does not necessarily mean:

    identical parse trees.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
15. MULTIPLE LEFT-RECURSIVE ALTERNATIVES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Suppose:

    A → Aα | Aβ | γ | δ


Here:

    Aα
    Aβ

are left-recursive alternatives.

Non-left-recursive alternatives:

    γ
    δ


Transformation:

    A  → γA' | δA'

    A' → αA' | βA' | ε


General pattern:

    A → Aα₁ | Aα₂ | β₁ | β₂

becomes:

    A  → β₁A' | β₂A'

    A' → α₁A' | α₂A' | ε


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
16. IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

For the standard immediate-left-recursion formula:

    A → Aα₁ | ... | β₁ | ...

the β alternatives should not begin with A.

If every alternative begins with A, the simple formula cannot directly
use a β as the terminating/base alternative.

You must first analyze whether the grammar can actually generate a
terminal string and whether another transformation is required.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
17. INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Left recursion can also occur through another non-terminal.

Example:

    A → Bα
    B → Aβ | γ


A does not immediately begin with A.

B does not immediately begin with B.

But:

    A ⇒ Bα
      ⇒ Aβα


Therefore A can derive a string beginning with A.

This is:

    INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION


Also called:

    INDIRECT / MUTUAL LEFT RECURSION


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
18. IMMEDIATE VS INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

IMMEDIATE:

    A → Aα


A directly calls itself.


INDIRECT:

    A → Bα

    B → Aβ


A reaches A through B.


Memory:

    A → Aα

        ↓

    Immediate


    A → B...
    B → A...

        ↓

    Indirect


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
19. EXAMPLE OF INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Grammar:

    A → B a | c
    B → A b | d


Start from A:

    A ⇒ Ba
      ⇒ Aba
      ⇒ ...


A derives something beginning with A.

Therefore there is indirect left recursion.


The grammar must be transformed before using a top-down parser that
cannot handle left recursion.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
20. GENERAL METHOD FOR REMOVING INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Suppose non-terminals are ordered:

    A₁, A₂, ..., Aₙ


For each Ai:

    1. Replace productions of the form

           Ai → Aj γ

       where j < i

       by substituting the productions of Aj.


    2. After substitution, remove any immediate left recursion
       from Ai.


Conceptually:

    ORDER NON-TERMINALS
          ↓
    SUBSTITUTE EARLIER NON-TERMINALS
          ↓
    REMOVE IMMEDIATE LEFT RECURSION
          ↓
    Repeat


This is the standard algorithmic approach.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
21. STEP-BY-STEP INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION IDEA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Suppose:

    A → Bx | y

    B → Az | w


Choose ordering:

    A, B


A has no earlier non-terminal, so process A.

For B:

    B → Az | w


Since A is earlier, substitute A:

    A → Bx | y

Therefore:

    B → (Bx | y)z | w


which becomes:

    B → Bxz | yz | w


Now B has immediate left recursion:

    B → Bxz | yz | w


Remove it using the standard formula.


The important idea is:

    INDIRECT → substitution → IMMEDIATE → remove.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
22. LEFT FACTORING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Another important grammar transformation is:

    LEFT FACTORING


It is useful when two or more productions have a common prefix.

Example:

    A → αβ₁ | αβ₂


Both alternatives begin with:

    α


A predictive parser may not know which alternative to choose by looking
at the available input prefix.

We factor the common prefix.


Transformation:

    A  → αA'

    A' → β₁ | β₂


This is called:

    LEFT FACTORING.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
23. SIMPLE LEFT-FACTORING EXAMPLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    S → if E then S else S
      | if E then S


Both alternatives begin with:

    if E then S


Factor it:

    S  → if E then S S'

    S' → else S | ε


Now the common prefix appears only once.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
24. ANOTHER LEFT-FACTORING EXAMPLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    A → abc | abd


Common prefix:

    ab


Factor:

    A  → abA'

    A' → c | d


This makes the choice easier for a predictive parser.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
25. WHY LEFT FACTORING IS NEEDED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Consider:

    A → αβ | αγ


A top-down parser sees:

    α


But it cannot immediately determine whether it should choose:

    αβ

or:

    αγ


Left factoring delays the decision until the distinguishing symbol is
visible.

Original:

    A → αβ | αγ


Transformed:

    A  → αA'
    A' → β | γ


Therefore:

    Common prefix → factor once
    Choice → postpone to A'


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
26. LEFT RECURSION VS LEFT FACTORING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

These are DIFFERENT transformations.

LEFT RECURSION:

    Problem:

        A → Aα | β


Purpose:

    Remove recursive expansion from the left side.


Transformation:

    A  → βA'
    A' → αA' | ε


LEFT FACTORING:

    Problem:

        A → αβ | αγ


Purpose:

    Remove common prefixes.


Transformation:

    A  → αA'
    A' → β | γ


Memory:

    LEFT RECURSION
        → removes A from beginning of its own production


    LEFT FACTORING
        → removes common prefix


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
27. GRAMMAR SIMPLIFICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Grammar simplification means removing unnecessary productions or
symbols while preserving the intended language as appropriate.

Common simplification targets:

    1. Useless symbols
    2. ε-productions
    3. Unit productions


These concepts are important in formal-language theory and grammar
processing.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
28. USELESS SYMBOLS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A grammar symbol may be useless if it contributes nothing to the
generation of terminal strings from the start symbol.

There are two important ideas:

    1. Non-generating symbols
    2. Unreachable symbols


A symbol is useful only if it is:

    • reachable from the start symbol
    AND
    • capable of deriving a terminal string.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
29. GENERATING SYMBOL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A non-terminal is generating if it can derive a string containing only
terminals.

Example:

    A → aA | b


A is generating because:

    A ⇒ b


Therefore:

    A

can eventually produce a terminal-only string.


Another example:

    B → C
    C → c


B is also generating:

    B ⇒ C ⇒ c


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
30. NON-GENERATING SYMBOL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Example:

    A → B
    B → A


Neither A nor B can derive a terminal string.

Therefore:

    A and B are non-generating.


If such symbols cannot contribute to any valid terminal derivation,
they are useless.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
31. REACHABLE SYMBOL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A symbol is reachable if it can occur in a derivation starting from
the start symbol.

Example:

    S → aA
    A → b


Both:

    S
    A

are reachable.


Suppose we also have:

    B → c


but no production from S or any reachable symbol can reach B.

Then:

    B

is unreachable.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
32. USELESS SYMBOL = NON-GENERATING OR UNREACHABLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A symbol can be useless if it is:

    • Non-generating
    OR
    • Unreachable


A standard simplification strategy is:

    Step 1:
        Remove non-generating symbols/productions.

    Step 2:
        Remove unreachable symbols/productions.


The exact order and implementation details matter in formal
algorithms, but this is the core idea.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
33. EXAMPLE — USELESS SYMBOL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Grammar:

    S → aA
    A → b
    B → c
    C → D
    D → C


Here:

    A generates b.

    B generates c but is unreachable from S.

    C and D cannot derive terminals.


Therefore:

    B → c

is unreachable.

    C, D

are non-generating.


After simplification, the useful grammar can be:

    S → aA
    A → b


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
34. ε-PRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

An ε-production is a production of the form:

    A → ε


It allows A to disappear from the sentential form.


Example:

    S → aA
    A → ε


Then:

    S ⇒ aA
      ⇒ a


Therefore:

    A can generate the empty string.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
35. NULLABLE NON-TERMINAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A non-terminal is NULLABLE if it can derive ε.

Example:

    A → ε


Therefore:

    A is nullable.


More complex example:

    A → BC
    B → ε
    C → ε


Then:

    A ⇒ BC
      ⇒ εε
      ⇒ ε


Therefore:

    A is also nullable.


Important:

    Nullability can propagate through productions.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
36. FINDING NULLABLE SYMBOLS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Example:

    S → AB
    A → a | ε
    B → b | ε


Initially:

    A is nullable.
    B is nullable.


Since:

    S → AB

and both A and B can disappear:

    S is nullable.


Therefore:

    Nullable = {A, B, S}


This type of reasoning appears frequently in GATE questions.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
37. UNIT PRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A unit production has the form:

    A → B


where both A and B are non-terminals.


Example:

    S → A
    A → B
    B → b


The first two are unit productions:

    S → A

    A → B


The final:

    B → b

is not a unit production because b is a terminal.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
38. REMOVING UNIT PRODUCTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Suppose:

    A → B

and:

    B → b | c


Then instead of:

    A → B
    B → b | c


we can replace the unit production by:

    A → b | c


while retaining B's own productions as needed.


More generally, unit-production elimination propagates the
non-unit productions reachable through chains of unit productions.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
39. UNIT-PRODUCTION CHAIN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Example:

    A → B
    B → C
    C → d | e


There is a unit chain:

    A ⇒ B ⇒ C


Therefore A can derive:

    d
    e


After removing the unit chain:

    A → d | e

can represent those direct possibilities.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
40. ε-PRODUCTION VS UNIT PRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ε-production:

    A → ε


Unit production:

    A → B


where B is a non-terminal.


Remember:

    ε-production → produces empty string

    Unit production → maps one non-terminal to another


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
41. SIMPLIFICATION OVERVIEW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Common grammar simplification operations:

    ┌─────────────────────────────┐
    │ Grammar Simplification      │
    └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                   │
       ┌───────────┼───────────┐
       ↓           ↓           ↓
   Useless       ε-rules     Unit-rules
   symbols     A → ε         A → B


The objective is to obtain a cleaner grammar while preserving the
relevant language.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
42. ORDER OF GRAMMAR TRANSFORMATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

There is no single universal "one order" for every compiler task.

For designing a predictive parser, a common workflow is:

    Start with CFG
          ↓
    Remove ambiguity if necessary
          ↓
    Remove left recursion
          ↓
    Apply left factoring if necessary
          ↓
    Compute FIRST/FOLLOW
          ↓
    Check LL(1)
          ↓
    Construct parsing table


For formal grammar simplification, the workflow may instead involve:

    Remove ε-productions
          ↓
    Remove unit productions
          ↓
    Remove useless symbols


The exact sequence can depend on the transformation algorithm and
the goal.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
43. IMPORTANT: AMBIGUITY IS NOT THE SAME AS LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This is a very common mistake.

LEFT RECURSION:

    A → Aα | β


AMBIGUITY:

    Some string has multiple distinct parse trees.


A grammar can be:

    • Left-recursive but unambiguous.
    • Ambiguous but not left-recursive.
    • Both.
    • Neither.


Therefore:

    Left recursion ≠ ambiguity.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
44. IMPORTANT: LEFT FACTORING DOES NOT REMOVE LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Left factoring handles:

    A → αβ | αγ


It does NOT automatically solve:

    A → Aα | β


Similarly, removing left recursion does not automatically perform
left factoring.


They solve different problems.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
45. EXAMPLE — BOTH TRANSFORMATIONS MAY BE NEEDED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Suppose grammar contains:

    A → Aα | βγ | βδ


There are two problems:

    1. Left recursion:
        A → Aα

    2. Common prefix:
        βγ | βδ


Possible strategy:

    First remove left recursion:

        A  → βγA' | βδA'
        A' → αA' | ε


Then left-factor:

        A  → βA'A''

or use carefully chosen new non-terminals to preserve the structure.

The exact resulting grammar depends on the desired factoring and
naming of auxiliary symbols.

Important principle:

    Identify each problem separately.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
46. GRAMMAR TRANSFORMATION AND LL(1)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

LL(1) means approximately:

    First L:
        input scanned from Left to right.

    Second L:
        construct a Leftmost derivation.

    1:
        use one lookahead symbol.


A grammar suitable for predictive parsing generally should not have
problematic left recursion.

It may also need left factoring when alternatives have common prefixes.


Therefore:

    Grammar Transformation
             ↓
       Parser Friendly
             ↓
          LL(1) analysis


Chapter 11 will study:

    FIRST
    FOLLOW
    LL(1)
    Predictive Parsing


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
47. EXAMPLE — FROM LEFT RECURSIVE TO PREDICTIVE FORM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Original:

    E → E + T | T


Problem:

    Left recursion.


Transform:

    E  → T E'
    E' → + T E' | ε


Now a parser can begin by parsing:

    T

and then repeatedly handle:

    + T


Conceptually:

    E
    ↓
    T
    ↓
    E'
    ├── + T E'
    └── ε


This structure is much more suitable for predictive parsing.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
48. REPEATED OPERATOR PATTERN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The transformation:

    A → Aα | β


becomes:

    A  → βA'
    A' → αA' | ε


This is equivalent to:

    A → βα*


at the language-pattern level.


This is an important conceptual connection.

Left recursion often represents:

    one base element + zero or more repetitions.


For example:

    E → E + T | T

corresponds conceptually to:

    T (+T)*


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
49. GATE-LEVEL EXAMPLE — REMOVE LEFT RECURSION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Given:

    A → Aa | Ab | c | d


Identify:

    α₁ = a
    α₂ = b

    β₁ = c
    β₂ = d


Apply:

    A  → cA' | dA'

    A' → aA' | bA' | ε


Answer:

    A  → cA' | dA'
    A' → aA' | bA' | ε


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
50. GATE-LEVEL EXAMPLE — LEFT FACTORING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Given:

    S → abc | abd | aef


Common prefix:

    a


Factor:

    S → aS'

Then:

    S' → bc | bd | ef


Can factor further because:

    bc | bd

share:

    b


So:

    S' → bS'' | ef

    S'' → c | d


This demonstrates that left factoring may need to be applied
REPEATEDLY.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
51. GATE TRAP — LONGEST COMMON PREFIX
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Given:

    A → abc | abd | aef


Do not stop after finding:

    a


if a longer common prefix exists among a subset of alternatives.

For:

    abc
    abd

the common prefix is:

    ab


A careful left-factoring transformation may be:

    A → abA' | aef

    A' → c | d


Then:

    A → aA''

    A'' → bA' | ef


with:

    A' → c | d


The exact form may vary, but the objective is to remove common
decision prefixes.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
52. GATE TRAP — LEFT RECURSION DETECTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Consider:

    A → BA
    B → A | b


At first glance, A does not directly start with A.

But:

    A ⇒ BA
      ⇒ AA


Therefore recursion can occur through B.

This is indirect left recursion.


Do not check only the first production symbol literally.

You may need to analyze derivations.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
53. GATE TRAP — UNIT PRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Which is a unit production?

    A → B


Yes.

Which is NOT?

    A → aB


Not unit.

Which is NOT?

    A → ε


Not unit.


Definition:

    Unit production = one non-terminal on each side:

        A → B


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
54. GATE TRAP — NULLABLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Grammar:

    A → BC
    B → ε
    C → c


Is A nullable?

No.

Why?

    B can disappear.

    C cannot disappear.

Therefore:

    BC ⇒ C

not:

    ε


For:

    A → BC
    B → ε
    C → ε


A is nullable.


Very important:

    All symbols on the relevant right-hand side must be able to
    disappear for that production to derive ε.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
55. GATE TRAP — REACHABLE VS GENERATING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A symbol can be:

    Generating but unreachable.

Example:

    S → a
    B → b


B can generate:

    b

but cannot be reached from S.

Therefore:

    B = generating
    B = unreachable

Hence B is useless for the language generated from S.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
56. GATE TRAP — LANGUAGE VS GRAMMAR
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Two grammars can generate the same language but have different:

    • Productions
    • Parse trees
    • Derivation sequences
    • Suitability for parsers


Therefore:

    Same language ≠ same grammar.


Grammar transformations often exploit this fact.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Remove left recursion     Left factoring

    CFG theory

    Practical parser construction.

58. COMMON GRAMMAR TRANSFORMATIONS — SUMMARY TABLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

    Problem                  Transformation
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Immediate left recursion A → Aα | β
                             ↓
                             A → βA'
                             A' → αA' | ε

    Common prefix             A → αβ | αγ
                             ↓
                             A → αA'
                             A' → β | γ

    ε-production              A → ε
                             → eliminate carefully when
                               simplifying grammar

    Unit production           A → B
                             → substitute B's non-unit
                               alternatives as appropriate

                             → remove

60. INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Q1. What is left recursion?

Answer:

    A grammar is left-recursive when a non-terminal can derive a string
    whose leftmost symbol is that same non-terminal. Immediate
    left recursion has the direct form A → Aα.


Q2. Why is left recursion a problem for recursive descent parsing?

Answer:

    A naive recursive-descent parser may repeatedly call the same
    non-terminal without consuming input, causing infinite recursion.


Q3. Can LR parsers handle left recursion?

Answer:

    Yes. Left-recursive grammars are commonly suitable for bottom-up
    LR-family parsing.


Q4. How do you remove immediate left recursion?

Answer:

    For:

        A → Aα₁ | ... | Aαₘ | β₁ | ... | βₙ

    use:

        A  → β₁A' | ... | βₙA'

        A' → α₁A' | ... | αₘA' | ε


Q5. What is left factoring?

Answer:

    Left factoring extracts a common prefix from alternatives so that
    a predictive parser can delay the choice until enough input has
    been seen.


Q6. What is indirect left recursion?

Answer:

    Left recursion that occurs through one or more other non-terminals.


Q7. What is a unit production?

Answer:

    A production of the form A → B, where A and B are non-terminals.


Q8. What is an ε-production?

Answer:

    A production of the form A → ε.


Q9. What is a nullable non-terminal?

Answer:

    A non-terminal that can derive ε.


Q10. What is a useless symbol?

Answer:

    A symbol that is not useful for deriving terminal strings from the
    start symbol, typically because it is non-generating or unreachable.

62. CHAPTER 10 — ONE-PAGE REVISION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

LEFT RECURSION:

    A → Aα | β


REMOVE:

    A  → βA'
    A' → αA' | ε


INDIRECT LEFT RECURSION:

    A → B...
    B → A...

Remove using:

    substitution
        ↓
    immediate left recursion removal


LEFT FACTORING:

    A → αβ | αγ

becomes:

    A  → αA'
    A' → β | γ


NULLABLE:

    A ⇒* ε


ε-PRODUCTION:

    A → ε


UNIT PRODUCTION:

    A → B


GENERATING:

    Can derive terminal-only string.


REACHABLE:

    Can be reached from start symbol.


USELESS:

    Non-generating or unreachable.


IMPORTANT:

    Left recursion ≠ ambiguity

    Left factoring ≠ left-recursion removal

    ε ≠ ∅

    Unit production ≠ ε-production

The key reason for grammar transformation is:

    Make the grammar more suitable for the parser we want to build.


The most important transformation for top-down parsing is:

    LEFT-RECURSION REMOVAL


The most important transformation for predictive choice is:

    LEFT FACTORING


The most important simplification concepts are:

    ε-productions
    Unit productions
    Useless symbols


The complete relationship is:

    CFG
     ↓
    Analyze grammar
     ↓
    Remove problematic left recursion
     ↓
    Left-factor when required
     ↓
    Simplify where appropriate
     ↓
    FIRST / FOLLOW
     ↓
    LL(1) analysis
     ↓
    Predictive parser
